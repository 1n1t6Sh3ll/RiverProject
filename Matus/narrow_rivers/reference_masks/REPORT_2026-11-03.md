## 1. Goal

Decided at the 2026-11-03 meeting:

1. Build a summer water mask that also keeps the **narrow channels**, as a proxy for
   where the river is.
2. Use it to separate **ice from snow** on the freeze-up date: frozen surface on the river
   is ice, frozen surface on land is snow.
3. Pick the **VIIRS 375 m cells** that can be trusted, and perfect them by hand in QGIS.
4. Run a **first U-Net** on VIIRS with those cells as targets.

## 2. What we did, in order

### Summer water mask (2024-06-22)

| Step | Result | Decision |

### Which VIIRS cells can be trusted

- **Geolocation.** The scene-wide Landsat–VIIRS match (method of `add_viirs.py`, refined to
  10 m steps) suggests 60 m W / 50 m N. But the correlation is 0.355 against 0.347 with no
  shift, which is barely different. Matching the **water mask itself** against VIIRS NIR/SWIR
  finds **no offset beyond noise**, and applying the scene-wide shift made the water-fraction
  match with VIIRS NIR worse (r −0.572 → −0.514).
  → **No shift applied.** The remaining uncertainty is ±40 m E-W, ±50 m N-S.
- **"Channel stays in its cell" test.** Our VIIRS grid is in degrees, so a cell is only
  ~162 m E-W × 374 m N-S. A narrow channel near a cell's E or W edge may fall in the
  neighbouring cell from VIIRS's point of view. A cell is usable if it keeps at least half
  of its water when the mask is shifted by the uncertainty in any of 8 directions. This test
  is relative, so it treats a 3 % cell the same as a 60 % one.
- **Result, June:** 547 of 1,224 cells containing water are usable; 66 of them are fully
  hand-labelled.

### First U-Net (pipeline test)

- **Setup:** input VIIRS I1–I5 for the whole box (132 × 80 cells, z-scored per image);
  target = the four class fractions per cell. The loss counts only the 131 checked cells
  (42 with river or ice, 89 pure land; 41 / 90 before the 11-04 re-run).
- **Split:** 75/25 by blocks of 4 × 4 cells, so test cells are not neighbours of training
  cells. That gives 98 train and 33 test cells, 10–11 of which contain river or ice.
- **Model:** 3-level U-Net, width 16, 118k parameters, 1,500 steps on CPU (under a minute).
- **Baselines on the same split:** always predict the training mean, and a random forest
  on each cell's own 5 VIIRS bands (VIIRS per-cell RF, *not* the Landsat RF, which made the
  targets).

Test error in the river + ice fraction (MAE), 5 different splits. Current runs
(`unet/runs/20261104_*`, targets from the 11-04 exports):

| | seed 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | **mean** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Training mean | 0.200 | 0.215 | 0.198 | 0.210 | 0.235 | **0.212** |
| VIIRS per-cell RF | 0.112 | 0.137 | 0.119 | 0.157 | 0.167 | **0.138** |
| U-Net | 0.048 | 0.105 | 0.130 | 0.088 | 0.100 | **0.094** |

First runs (`unet/runs/20261103_*`, 11-03 targets): training mean 0.197, VIIRS per-cell RF
0.129, U-Net 0.114.

On its own training cells the U-Net's error is about 0.006, so it memorises them.

## 3. What we achieved with a very small sample

- **The whole chain works end to end:** Landsat labels → RF → summer mask (+ Sentinel-2,
  overrides) → channel rule → hand-checked VIIRS cells → 375 m fractions → U-Net on VIIRS.
- **The reference data is good.** On the freeze-up date, the RF + channel rule needed no
  corrections in the 41–42 cells checked by eye, before and after the June re-run. The
  channel rule fixes most of the ice/snow confusion: snow recall goes from 0.45 to 0.89.
- **The U-Net learns something real.** Its error is less than half of the naive baseline
  (0.094 vs 0.212) and it rarely puts river on land. It is now **somewhat better than a VIIRS
  per-cell random forest** on average (0.094 vs 0.138), but the splits overlap (U-Net
  0.05–0.13, RF 0.11–0.17), so this is not yet a firm win. With the 11-03 targets the two
  were on par (0.114 vs 0.129).
- **What limits it is checked data, not the model:** 131 cells in one corner of one date,
  only 10–11 river/ice cells per test split. The seed-0 result (0.048) is a lucky split.
- **Outside the checked corner the U-Net predicts river or ice almost everywhere.** It has
  never seen those areas, which is another sign it needs more checked cells.

## 4. How to replicate on another machine

### What you need
- The `Matus/` venv (Python 3.13) with `requirements.txt`. PyTorch is the CPU build:
  `pip install torch==2.15.0 --index-url https://download.pytorch.org/whl/cpu`.
- **Earth Engine** access to project `noaa-river-ice` (`earthengine authenticate`), used for
  Landsat, Sentinel-2 and NLCD.
- **JRC tiles** in `Source/EarthEngineExports/`, and the **VIIRS granules** for the dates in
  `narrow_rivers/data/viirs/` (download as in `narrow_rivers/README.md` step 5).

### What is in git and what is not
- **Not in git, regenerated by the scripts:** every raster (`*.tif`), `*.csv`, VIIRS granules (`*.h5`).
- **In git, and cannot be regenerated:** the hand-drawn layers.
  - `patches/nenana_*/labels/labels_<date>.shp`
  - `reference_masks/<date>/overrides/overrides_<date>.shp`
  - `reference_masks/<date>/done/done_<date>.shp`
- Also in git: run records (`runs/*/config.json`), GeoJSON cell layers, QGIS styles, and
  U-Net `model.pt` files.

- `reference_masks\geoloc_offset.py --date <date>`: offset with direction.
- `union_mask.py --source jrc`: the rejected JRC variants.
- `train_rf.py --error-weight 3`: the error-weighting test.

### Rules the scripts follow
- Hand-drawn layers are only ever **read**. `make_base.py` creates the empty override and
  done layers once and never touches them again.
- `apply_overrides.py` writes a **dated snapshot** and never overwrites; a second export on
  the same day gets `_HHMM`.
- Priority in the final mask: **override > hand label > model**. Each cell records how
  much came from each (`f_hand`, `f_override`, `f_model`).
- A cell counts as **done** when its **centre** lies inside a done polygon.
- Class codes everywhere: 1 water/river, 2 ice, 3 snow, 4 land, 9 unsure.
